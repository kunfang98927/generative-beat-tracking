Can Music Generation Models Tap Along? Evaluating Generative Approaches to Beat Tracking

Anonymous authors

Summary

We add song-driven conditioning to a pretrained MusicGen so that it generates a percussion track that plays along with a song, and we then extract the beats from this track with a small beat tracker. This page shows examples of these generated percussion tracks and compares their beats with those of other generative and traditional beat tracking methods.

2. Model-generated percussion tracks and beat tracking results

Five songs from the test set, which no model saw during training or model selection. In each song, use the Input switch to compare the two inputs: the full mix, or the song with its drums removed by source separation. Click a row name to play that row; the generated tracks are played together with the input song, and the Song / Percussion slider sets the balance between them. Drag the bar above the plot to move in time.

  • Ours: MusicGen-small conditioned on the song through a small adapter (MusicGen-large reads the song), with one model per target: Click, Groove (synth) and Groove (sampled). Ours (w/o DR aug)* is the Groove (sampled) or Groove (synth) model trained without drum-removal augmentation.
  • Stable Audio ControlNet: a diffusion model (Stable Audio Open) conditioned on the song through a ControlNet branch, trained on the same songs and the Groove (sampled) target.
  • STAGE: an existing drum accompaniment model (MusicGen-small, fully fine-tuned) trained on real drum stems.
  • Beat This! and madmom: traditional beat tracking models (a transformer, and an RNN with a DBN) that output beat positions directly; their rows play a click on each predicted beat.

The beats of each generated track are extracted with a small TCN beat tracker trained for that generative model.

Appendix: the Groove targets

  • Groove (synth): synthesized percussion (kick, snare, hi-hat, cowbell, conga, …) played with a rule-based rhythm pattern chosen by the genre of the song. There are 14 patterns, each genre is mapped to one fixed pattern (notated below), and music without drums gets hand claps.
  • Groove (sampled): real drum performances instead of fixed patterns. For each song, we take a human drum performance from the Groove MIDI Dataset that matches the song's genre and tempo, align it to the annotated beats bar by bar, and play it with real drum-kit samples. Dance styles the dataset lacks (tango, samba, waltzes) use the synth pattern played with real samples, and music without drums gets orchestral percussion.
